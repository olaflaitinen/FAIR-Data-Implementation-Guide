## Slide 2: The Data Crisis
- **Problem**: 80% of scientific data is lost within 20 years.
- **Cause**: Poor documentation, obsolete formats, lack of storage.
- **Consequence**: Irreproducible research and wasted funding.
- **Solution**: Better data stewardship.

---

## Slide 3: What is FAIR?
**F**indable: Can machines and humans find it?
**A**ccessible: Can they get to it?
**I**nteroperable: Can they combine it with other data?
**R**eusable: Can they use it for new research?

*Note: FAIR does not necessarily mean "Open". Data can be FAIR but restricted (e.g., patient data).*

---

## Slide 4: Findable (F)
- **F1**: Persistent Identifiers (DOIs, ORCIDs).
- **F2**: Rich Metadata (Who, What, When, Where, How).
- **F3**: Explicit Linkage (Metadata contains the Data ID).
- **F4**: Searchable Repositories (Zenodo, GenBank).

*Analogy: A book in a library needs a catalog entry (metadata) and a call number (PID) to be found.*

---

## Slide 5: Accessible (A)
- **A1**: Standard Protocols (HTTP, FTP).
- **A1.1**: Open & Free (No proprietary software needed to download).
- **A1.2**: Authentication (Login allowed, but process must be clear).
- **A2**: Metadata Persistence (Metadata stays even if data goes).

*Analogy: You might need a library card (auth) to borrow the book, but the catalog entry is always free to view.*

---

## Slide 6: Interoperable (I)
- **I1**: Formal Languages (JSON-LD, RDF, XML).
- **I2**: FAIR Vocabularies (Ontologies like GO, ChEBI).
- **I3**: Qualified References (Links to other papers/data).

*Analogy: The book is written in a language (English/French) and uses standard grammar so others can read it.*

---

## Slide 7: Reusable (R)
- **R1**: Rich Context (Detailed methods, units, variables).
- **R1.1**: Clear License (CC-BY, MIT).
- **R1.2**: Provenance (History of the data).
- **R1.3**: Community Standards (MIAME, BIDS).

*Analogy: The book has a clear copyright page and citations so you know how you can quote it.*

---

## Slide 8: Practical Steps for You
1. **Plan**: Write a Data Management Plan (DMP) *before* you start.
2. **Organize**: Use consistent file naming and folder structures.
3. **Describe**: Document your metadata as you go (don't wait until the end).
4. **Publish**: Deposit final datasets in a trusted repository.
